The various incarnations of Valentin Tomberg

by Robert Powell

A Talk given by Dr Robert Powell on 12 December 2025, in Ecuador.

[transcribed from a talk given on a podcast.]

Today is the 12th December 2025. We are going to continue with looking into the previous incarnations of Valentin Tomberg or, as he was referred to by Rudolf Steiner, the Bodhisattva who will become the Maitreya Buddha.

As spoken of previously, Valentin Tomberg was born on 26th February 1900, which coincides exactly with Rudolf Steiner’s indication that the Bodhisattva who will become the Maitreya Buddha was born around the beginning of the twentieth century. In 1921 Rudolf Steiner said to Friedrich Rittelmeyer that if we live another fifteen years (which takes us to the mid-1930s) we will notice the activity of this Bodhisattva. And in earlier lectures on the Gospel of St Matthew, Rudolf Steiner had spoken about this Bodhisattva who will become the Maitreya Buddha and said that he would be the ‘real proclaimer’ of Christ’s Second Coming, His coming in the etheric.

Rudolf Steiner mentioned the years 1930 and 1933 as the beginning of Christ’s Second Coming. Thus, in order for this to be a real proclamation, this Bodhisattva individuality would have had to make the proclamation after the beginning of the 1930s. Valentin Tomberg was born in 1900 and began speaking about Christ’s Second Coming shortly after the beginning of the 1930s. We can say, then, that Valentin Tomberg fulfilled these two requirements indicated by Rudolf Steiner: that this individuality would be born at the beginning of the twentieth century, and that he would begin speaking about Christ’s Second Coming during the 1930s.

It is not just through the fulfilment of these two requirements, but also through the research I was doing into this individuality that I could say with a hundred percent certainty that Valentin Tomberg is this individuality, otherwise I would not speak about this.

Rudolf Steiner spoke of this Bodhisattva who would become the next Buddha, the Maitreya Buddha. Rudolf Steiner said that Gautama Buddha died round about the year 480 BC. We do not know when Gautama attained enlightenment, but it is said that he attained enlightenment in his thirtieth year and died when he was about the age of eighty. And so he could have attained enlightenment some time before the year 500 BC. If we add 5000 years to this, we come to the middle of the fifth millennium, about the year 4500—it could be before or after this time, we cannot say for certain—when this Bodhisattva individuality, the successor of Gautama Buddha, will become the next Buddha.

We have been speaking about the community of the great teachers, the circle of twelve around Christ. Seven of them are referred to as the ‘seven Holy Rishis’ who were together with the one who is called the Manu in the Hindu tradition, or Noah in the Old Testament tradition, or also as Melchizedek. As is described in the Bible before the Flood began, which destroyed Atlantis, this great individuality, the Manu, built the ark—Noah’s Ark as we know it—and he took his disciples, including the seven Holy Rishis, to safety so that they did not go under with the destruction of Atlantis through the great Flood. Atlantis was an island in the region where the Atlantic Ocean is now.

The Manu led his disciples to the region in the Himalayas which is referred to now as the Gobi desert. At that time it was not a desert. It was a flourishing valley in the sacred mountains. That is where they lived. Then the Manu said that it was time for the seven Holy Rishis (who were his disciples) to go to northern India, which is where they went to found a new culture. This is what Rudolf Steiner refers to as the first cultural epoch which followed upon the destruction of Atlantis.

Each cultural epoch corresponds to a sign of the zodiac. It is not identical with the zodiacal sign, but arises during the unfolding of that zodiacal sign. In other words, when the vernal point enters a new zodiacal sign there is the beginning of a new zodiacal age. However, it takes some time—approximately 1200 years—before the new culture is born. We can follow this pattern through time from the first cultural epoch of ancient India (approximately 7000 BC), then with the following second cultural epoch—the ancient Persian—and then with the third cultural epoch, that of the Egypto-Babylonian culture. We are now up to round about 3000 BC. Then there was the beginning of the fourth cultural epoch. Rudolf Steiner gave a specific date for the beginning of the fourth cultural epoch—he mentioned the year 747 BC as the founding of the ancient City of Rome. The fourth cultural epoch began at the time of the founding of Rome.

Each cultural epoch lasts for 2160 years, which is the length of time taken for the vernal point to go through one sign of the zodiac. If we add 2160 years to 747 BC, we come to the year 1414 AD, which is the beginning of our fifth cultural epoch. This was the time of the beginning of the flourishing of European culture with the Renaissance in Italy, and the appearance of scientific thinking which is characteristic of this fifth cultural epoch.

The fifth cultural epoch will last for 2160 years. If we add 2160 to the year 1414, we come to the year 3574, which is the beginning of the next, the sixth, cultural epoch that will arise within the age of Aquarius. The age of Aquarius itself begins when the vernal point enters Aquarius in the year 2375. Then 1200 years later—if we go forwards from the year 2375 and add 1200 years (it is actually 1199 years)—we come to the year 3574. The year 3574 is the beginning of the sixth cultural epoch within the age of Aquarius. If we go forwards to round about the end of the sixth cultural epoch, that is approximately when the Maitreya will become the next Buddha.

Going back in time, this Bodhisattva was incarnated at the time of Gautama Buddha, and had many incarnations before then. I mentioned some of them last time. The oldest incarnation that we know of is as one of the seven Holy Rishis. His name was Kashyapa, and he is referred to as the most ancient and venerated Rishi—along with the other Rishis—in India. They were known as the Saptarishis, which means the ‘Seven Holy Rishis.’

I am going to read out some words that are attributed to Kashyapa from the Atharvaveda:

[Kali or] Kala (meaning Time) created yonder heaven and Kala made these realms of earth.

By Kala, stirred to motion, both what is and what shall be, expand…

Kala created living things and first of all Prajapati [Prajapati is the Son of Brahma].

From Kala self-made Kashyapa, from Kala Holy Fire was born.1

Kashyapa is mentioned in numerous Hindu texts, for example in the Puranas.

Kashyapa also incarnated as Vasudeva who is said to be the father of Krishna. However, according to Rudolf Steiner, Krishna did not actually physically incarnate but he overlighted Arjuna. In the Hindu tradition Vasudeva is regarded as having a very close connection with Krishna, and is referred to as the father of Krishna. And, according to Rudolf Steiner, Krishna had only one incarnation upon the Earth—that was as Jesus of Nazareth. And so we can see from this that Kashyapa has a very close connection with Jesus of Nazareth.

There was a subsequent incarnation of Kashyapa—after this ancient Indian incarnation—as Abraham who was the founder of the Jewish people. With the founding of the Jewish people, Abraham was visited by the Manu or—as he is referred to in the Bible—Melchizedek who brought bread and wine to Abraham. With Abraham there thus began what is called the Feast of Love or, in the Greek language, Agape. This was transmitted down from the time of Abraham through his son Isaac, and through Isaac to his son Jacob, and through Jacob to his son Joseph, and so on, all the way down to the time of Christ. As we know, Christ celebrated this Feast, or Festival, of the Agape, but in a different form to that in which Melchizedek celebrated this for Abraham. The new form in which Christ celebrated this Feast is what we know of as the Eucharist or the Mass, and this goes all the way back to Melchizedek, the Manu.

To understand who Melchizedek is, we can say that he is not a human being but an angelic being who, however, was able to come into human form as Melchizedek, or earlier as Noah. He is the great guide of the Bodhisattvas, or the Holy Rishis, or however you’d like to call these great beings

These Bodhisattvas, or Rishis, incarnate at different points in time and are often the founders of religions. Gautama was a Bodhisattva who attained Buddhahood. With the attainment of Buddhahood, this meant that it was no longer necessary for him to incarnate upon the Earth. But Buddha continues to work from spiritual realms.

As mentioned earlier, Kashyapa reincarnated as Vasudeva who is said to be the father of Krishna. Krishna did actually incarnate as Jesus of Nazareth. And so we can see this very close connection between these two individualities. Abraham was preparing for the coming of Krishna who incarnated as Jesus of Nazareth. It was the mission of the Jewish people to give birth to the perfect human being—this incarnation of Krishna as Jesus of Nazareth—and they fulfilled this mission. Krishna himself is a Bodhisattva, the Bodhisattva who corresponds to the sign of Pisces.

If we go forwards in time from the incarnation of Abraham—we do not know exactly when Abraham lived – some time after the year 2000 BC—then we come to an incarnation of this individuality at the time of Gautama Buddha. As a Bodhisattva, this individuality who had been incarnated as Kashyapa came to join the community founded by the great Bodhisattva Gautama. Gautama, who became Buddha, recognised that this individuality was the reincarnated Kashyapa who was one of the seven Holy Rishis, and Buddha gave him a new name—which was not the name his parents had given him—that of Mahākāśyapa. In Hindu Mahā means “great.” Gautama Buddha then indicated that Mahākāśyapa would be the next Buddha. After the death of Buddha, Mahākāśyapa assumed the leadership of the Buddha’s monastic community. There were about one thousand followers in this monastic community and Mahākāśyapa presided over this community. He called the first Buddhist Council because they were concerned how Buddhism could be communicated in the right way for people.

And so Mahākāśyapa was given this name by Gautama Buddha who recognised him as an enlightened one, a Bodhisattva who had been incarnated as the Holy Rishi Kashyapa during the ancient Indian epoch. As Gautama Buddha recognised this individuality as the Holy Rishi, Kashyapa, Gautama gave him the name Mahākāśyapa who was Gautama Buddha’s enlightened disciple, being foremost in the ascetic practice that Gautama taught.

Thus after Buddha’s death, Mahākāśyapa became the guide of this community founded by Gautama Buddha and then, after the death of Mahākāśyapa, Ānanda became the next leader of this community. Ānanda was the cousin of Gautama Buddha and was well known for his great capacity of memory. He could remember everything that the Buddha said. Whatever writings were taken down later as the words of Gautama Buddha are from Ānanda.

Ānanda himself is a very interesting figure. Ānanda reincarnated as one of the twelve disciples of Jesus of Nazareth. This is the disciple we know of as John, the brother of James. John, the reincarnated Ānanda, was a very kind, devoted and loving individual. John, his brother James, and Peter were often taken on special occasions by Jesus—for example, the three of them were witness to the Enlightenment or the Transfiguration of Jesus on Mount Tabor. It was Peter, James and John who Jesus of Nazareth took with Him up Mount Tabor where the Transfiguration took place. It was about midnight when they arrived at the summit of Mount Tabor and, shortly after, the Transfiguration took place. The fact that John was generally included, together with James and Peter, on these special occasions is a sign of how close John was to Jesus.

We can thus see how Buddha was preparing for the coming of Jesus. It is the Buddhist teachings of compassion, above all, that we associate with Buddha. It was this teaching of compassion that was preparing the way for Jesus who also manifested compassion, mercy and goodness. And so we can see that this stream, connected with Buddha, came down to Jesus Christ.

Directly before the coming of Jesus, there was an incarnation of Kashyapa—about one hundred years before Christ—as Jeshu ben Pandira. The only incarnation spoken of by Rudolf Steiner of this individuality was of Jeshu ben Pandira, the founder of the Essene community located at Qumran north-west of the Dead Sea. It was here where the Essene community lived and where this individuality, Jeshu ben Pandira, taught. Later, in some caves near the community at Qumran, many of the teachings of Jeshu ben Pandira were found. These teachings, which had been written down, were found in scrolls in some caves also near the Dead Sea. If you go to that area of Qumran, you find that this was where the scrolls were found. They were found in the period between 1947 and 1956. It took quite some time before they were translated and published. I am not sure of the exact date when the English translation of the Dead Sea Scrolls was published, but it was round about 1980. We know of this individuality of Jeshu ben Pandira primarily through these Dead Sea Scrolls.

I would like to read out some words of Estelle Isaacson, from a vision she had of Jeshu ben Pandira in 2014. It is titled: Jeshu ben Pandira and his Essene Community.

I saw Jeshu ben Pandira the great teacher of righteousness. The community had gathered around him to partake of his wisdom. This community assisted him when he passed through certain trials and initiations. They protected him and they were known as Essenes and lived in Qumran. Close by Qumran were caves where the Essenes would go to receive the teachings of Jeshu ben Pandira. They would sit together around a fire under the stars as he imparted his wisdom.

Jeshu ben Pandira bore within the burden of knowing that the time was coming when the Messiah would appear. He knew that the Messiah would come through both a priestly and a kingly lineage.

The birth of Jesus of Nazareth, in terms of the priestly lineage, is described in the Gospel of St Luke, and the kingly line is described in the Gospel of St Matthew. Rudolf Steiner was the first to clarify this. Of course the gospels did not exist at the time of Jeshu ben Pandira, but it is remarkable that he spoke about these two lines: the priestly line and the kingly line.

Jeshu ben Pandira knew these two streams would merge into one and he spoke of this to his disciples. This wisdom he received from the stars. He was very much attuned to the stars. To him the overarching patterns and rhythms, the spiritual order of the stars, was clear. He knew within his own being this deep ordering of the stars. He could think in concert with the stars. This is how he had received the heavenly message of the Messiah which was such a burden to his soul.

In those times the Essenes practised many rituals of cleansing: purifying ritual baths, dietary restrictions and so forth. All of these rituals and restrictions were kept by this community of Essenes but Jeshu ben Pandira taught them that there was a higher way, and they were initiated into this higher way.

The message of the priestly-kingly Messiah was shared with the whole community. It was given in such a way that the knowledge of who the Messiah would be, and the deed he would perform for all humanity, was inscribed in their hearts.

One cloudless night, Jeshu ben Pandira showed this to his people in the stars. He showed them how it was ordained in the heavens and their eyes of spirit were opened. They were ministered to by a mighty Archangel—all of them together. Each saw in spiritual vision the mighty Archangel Jesus descending out of heaven. Jeshu ben Pandira told his people to keep this revelation in their hearts and remember it in the coming times. He bade them remember it also in future life times.

Often the community met for teachings and discussions. At one of these gatherings a disciple asked: “Is he who is coming, the one prophesied to come to the Jews, the Mashiach?” [Mashiach is the Hebrew for the Messiah]. Jeshu said: “Yes, he is the Mashiach of the Jews. He will come but few will recognise him.” The disciples were awe-struck. Then another asked: “Might we return in our next incarnation and know him that we might serve the people? Should there not be in the body dwelling on Earth some who are looking for his coming, who can recognise him, and who can then go out and tell the people and gather them together?” Jeshu ben Pandira replied humbly, bowing his head and gently nodding: “Yes, some will choose to come then and they will know him.” Then another asked: “Could it be us Master?” And Jeshu ben Pandira answered: “You may choose to do so and, if you do so choose, you will know the greatest of all joys and you will also know the greatest suffering, for you will witness the deed he shall accomplish for all humanity.” And they asked: “Why will this cause such suffering Master?” And he answered: “Because you will love him and you will witness his suffering for all mankind, and then you will take up his work. You will bear that which he shall bear.”

Then Jeshu ben Pandira said: “I must deliver this message to the people now that we have held this mystery in our hearts and it has taken root. I must leave you for a time to go out and proclaim this message. Be with me in prayer that your strength may be my strength, that your peace may be my peace, that I may be strong in what I must do.”

Jeshu ben Pandira prepared for the journey for many days and then the community of disciples came together. They blessed and prayed for him and he blessed them in return and bade them farewell.

Now there is another gathering, a gathering for the Second Coming, and hearts shall be awakened to the word that is born within; the ancient word, the word of redemption and resurrection. This word shall be awakened and come forth through the new gathering of the community. It will resound in the ethers and touch hearts that those who know Christ may awaken and receive him, for the Second Coming will happen in the hearts of the people.

This gives us some idea of Jeshu ben Pandira, who Rudolf Steiner indicated was an incarnation of the Bodhisattva, who will become the Maitreya Buddha. It was more discreetly, and without putting a particular person in the limelight as candidate, that Dr. Rudolf Steiner, founder of the Anthroposophical Society, predicted the manifestation—again in the first half of the twentieth century—not of the new Maitreya Buddha or Kalki Avatar, but rather of the Bodhisattva, i.e. the individuality in the process of becoming the next Buddha, whose field of activity he hoped the Anthroposophical Society would serve…

In Letter XXI Valentin Tomberg says more about this. What is of interest for us is this reference to the Kalki Avatar. The Kalki Avatar is referred to in the Hindu tradition as the tenth Avatar. The Avatars are those who are enlightened or almost, one could say, bearers of the stream of the inspiration of Vishnu who, in the Hindu tradition, corresponds to Christ in the Christian tradition. In the Christian tradition there is the Trinity: the Father, the Son (Christ is the Son) and the Holy Spirit. In the Hindu tradition the Father is Brahman, the Son is Vishnu, and what we know of as the Holy Spirit in the Christian tradition is not quite the same as in the Hindu tradition. We could say, however, that in the Hindu tradition there is a sense for at least these three aspects of the Trinity.

In the sequence of Avatars, Rama (connected with the Hindu tradition) is referred to as the seventh Avatar. Krishna is referred to as the eighth Avatar. In a certain respect he also belongs to the Hindu tradition. Krishna came in about the year 3200 BC which was during the third cultural epoch, and so he is referred to as the eighth Avatar. Gautama Buddha, the enlightened one, is referred to as the ninth Avatar. And the tenth Avatar, Kalki, is now awaited.

Each Avatar is believed to have descended to restore cosmic order, with Kalki being the final one whose role is to bring an end to the Age of Darkness. Valentin Tomberg says that the Kalki Avatar and the Maitreya Buddha will be one and the same. He expresses this in a very curious way because he says that at that event they will be one and the same. There must be a very close connection. What this means is that there are essentially two aspects to this Bodhisattva who is both the Maitreya, the bearer of the good, and the Kalki Avatar who is the one who comes to restore cosmic order.

This is very interesting because, for the Essenes, Jeshu ben Pandira was referred to as the teacher of righteousness. And so we can see this connection.

In the Hindu tradition, according to the prophecies, Kalki will appear at a time when society has completely lost its way; when falsehood and unrighteousness prevail, and when people have abandoned their spiritual duties. The Vishnu Purana and the Bhagavata Purana describe Kalki’s arrival at the moment when the world is overwhelmed by unrighteousness and the forces of evil have gained control. It is at this point that Vishnu, in the form of Kalki, will descend to annihilate the wicked and restore the balance of the cosmos. And so we can see what an important figure the Kalki Avatar is.
