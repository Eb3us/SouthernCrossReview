# The Magic Mound - Chapter 23-24

![](magic-mound-cover.jpg){class="a"}

## by Frank Thomas Smith

### Chapter 23

### Three Wishes

Tamoshot sat down on the gnarled roots of a thick-trunked tree. The
three children sat cross-legged on the ground in front of him. The sun
filtered through the high treetops speckling the forest floor with its
light. Platero closed his eyes and dozed.

\"If you want to return home,\" Tamoshot said, \"I suggest you rest here
until dawn and then set out for the Magic Mound.\" He was completely
different from the Tamoshot they had first met a month ago behind a rock
in the mountains of Urmaxa. He was no longer a clown, he was more like a
king.

\"Without saying goodbye?\" Divino asked.

\"You would find it difficult to say goodbye to the Maxanians, for whom
you have done so much,\" Tamoshot replied.

\"Alaram wanted Sergio to explain something to the women,\" Mireya said.

\"Alaram will be able to explain himself now.\" Tamoshot\'s tone left no
room for discussion on this point.

\"Tamoshot?\"

\"Yes, my friend,\" he answered Sergio.

\"Who are you?\"

Tamoshot smiled. \"How curious you are. A Maxanian would never have
asked me that, at least not so directly.

\"Tamoshot told us who he was when we first met him,\" Divino said.
\"Don\'t you remember, Sergio?\"

\"But your wise brother does not believe the story I told you, and quite
right he is.\" Then, to Sergio, Tamoshot said, \"Who do *you* think I
am?\"

\"I don\'t know, but I think you have something to do with the god of
Maxa.\"

\"Yes, that is correct.\"

\"Are you the god\'s son, too?\" Divino asked him.

\"Too?\"

\"They said that Arman was his son.\"

\"We were all the god\'s children - Light, Arman and I,\" Tamoshot
explained. \"Of course all Maxanians are the god\'s children, but we
three were different because we were with him and saw him. And we had no
bodies like the Maxanians. Arman rebelled and became a Maxanian in order
to have power. Light remained a spirit, but she also disobeyed the god.
Then I also became a Maxanian, meaning that I took on a body, not for
the sake of power, but to save the Maxanians from Arman. They could not
have saved themselves, for he was too powerful. And I could not have
done it without you three - Sergio, Divino and Mireya.\"

\"And Platero,\" Divino added.

\"Of course, and Platero,\" Tamoshot acknowledged.

At the sound of his name the donkey opened one eye. Reassured that they
were still only talking, he closed it and went back to sleep.

\"But Tamoshot...\" Mireya began.

\"Don\'t worry, my child,\" Tamoshot interrupted, \"I will explain to
them that you had to leave right away and that I told you to do so. They
will understand.\"

\"Can we ever come back?\"

\"Perhaps. That depends on many things. You do deserve a reward
though.\" Tamoshot\'s eyes twinkled like distant stars. He waited for
them to react.

\"Do you mean that we can have three wishes?\" Divino asked, having
learned from Dona Ute\'s stories that wishes always come in threes.

Tamoshot smiled. \"Three wishes! My, that\'s a lot.\" He thought a
moment. \"But in fact you are right, you have three wishes, but you have
already had two of them.\"

\"We have?\" Divino exclaimed. \"\"What were they?\"

Tamoshot lifted his left hand and ticked of the wishes on his fingers.
\"One, that you may return to Brazil; two that Mireya may return with
you and attend the little school...\"

\"Then I\'ll be able to go to the little school?\" Mireya asked happily.

\"Let\'s just say that you made the wish. Now you have one left.\"

The three children looked at Tamoshot, then at each other. They didn\'t
know what else to wish for.

\"You have very little time to decide.\" Tamoshot said. \"I must go
soon.\"

\"Can you grant wishes in Brazil?\" Sergio asked suddenly.

\"Well, I have friends there,\" Tamoshot answered, suppressing a smile.

\"May I wish for us all?\" Sergio asked Mireya and Divino.

They nodded.

\"The little school is poor,\" he said. \"It\'s not much more than a
wooden shack and there isn\'t enough room for all the favela children
and no money to buy food or to pay teachers or for many other things
that Dona Ute needs, like a clinic with doctors and nurses, because many
favela people are sick.\" Sergio paused.

\"Yes?\" Tamoshot asked.

\"So I, we, wish for Dona Ute to receive what she needs to do these
things.\"

The little man in white raised his right hand and touched Sergio\'s
cheek with two fingers. Then he said, \"Your wish is good. Rest now. If
you leave early you should reach the mound by tomorrow evening.\"

\"How will we find it?\" Mireya asked.

\"I will send a guide. Now goodbye and thank you.\" Tamoshot turned and
walked quickly through the forest until he was lost to view.

The children were too tired to discuss the day\'s events, or even to
think about them. Divino lay down and rested his head on a root. It was
not a soft pillow, but he fell asleep before he had time to yawn. Mireya
and Sergio walked around the tree and found a comfortable place between
two roots. There they slept holding hands until the dawn.


### Chapter 24

### The White Stag and the Return to Brazil

Platero stood gazing into the deeper part of the forest and stamped his
hooves, waking the children. They followed his gaze and saw a
magnificent stag with huge antlers framed by the glow of the rising sun
watching them. Several moments passed in silence until the stag turned
and started to walk away.

Mireya jumped to her feet. \"It\'s Tamoshot\'s guide,\" she cried. \"He
wants us to follow him.\"

\"Are you sure?\" Sergio asked.

\"Almost, come on.\"

Mireya started to follow the stag and the others followed her. Sometimes
they lost sight of the stag, but not for long. He always reappeared to
show the way. They followed him all day without a pause until finally,
towards evening, they stood in front of the Magic Mound. The stag had
disappeared.

The three children, followed by Platero, climbed to the top of the
mound. The full moon had risen over the tree-tops and Sergio remembered
that it had also been full when they had first set foot on the Magic
Mound. Platero was the first to be affected by the mound\'s power: he
closed his eyes and slept. Sergio told Mireya and Divino to sit in the
chair-like indentations. He lay down on the soft earth and looked once
more at the moon before joining the others in a deep slumber.

When Sergio woke up the moon was still full, but it was behind instead
of in front of him as it had been when he fell asleep. He felt the
heaviness of the humid air and knew that they were back in Brazil.
Platero nudged him with his nose and he got to his feet.

\"Mireya, Divino, wake up, we\'re in Brazil!\"

Divino jumped up and, still groggy with sleep, ran forward, lost his
footing and rolled down the mound. The others laughed and Platero
brayed. Even Divino laughed, but when he tried to stand up he fell and
cried out in pain. Mireya examined his foot and determined that he had
sprained his ankle.

\"It\'s lucky we have Platero with us,\" she said. \"You can ride on
him.\"

They helped Divino up onto Platero\'s back and Sergio led the way out of
the circle of white stones surrounding the Magic Mound.

\"We have a long way to go,\" he said, \"down the mountain to Paraty,
then somehow to Sao Paulo.\"

\"I don\'t want to go to Paraty,\" Mireya said. She stopped walking and
looked at Sergio with fear in her eyes. \"That man might catch us.\"

\"Don\'t worry, Mireya,\" Sergio said, \"we\'ll be careful.\"

\"Platero is the only white donkey in Brazil,\" she insisted. \"He will
be seen and someone is sure to tell the man.\"

\"You\'re right, it could be a problem. I\'ll think of something.\"

\"Sergio will think of something, Mireya,\" Divino said with his usual
confidence in his brother. \"Don\'t worry, that man won\'t catch us.\"

\"We might be able to get into the house we left a month ago,\" Sergio
said, \"or at least sleep in the garden. It\'s near, let\'s go.\"

They soon reached the stream. Sergio and Mireya went across on the same
rocks that Divino had used once before. Platero, carrying Divino,
stepped delicately into the water, which barely touched his stomach, and
crossed easily to the other side. They followed the stream until they
came to the house. To their surprise, its lights were on.

\"We\'re in luck,\" Sergio said. The owner must be home. She\'s Dona
Ute\'s friend and will let us stay overnight.\"

\"As they approached the house their surprise turned first to joy and
then to astonishment when they heard Dona Ute\'s voice from inside:
\"What can be keeping those boys? Maybe you should go and look for them,
Zeca, they might be lost.\"

Then Zeca: \"Oh, let\'s wait a while. Sergio wouldn\'t get lost on such
a brightly moonlit evening. They\'re probably exploring.\"

\"Don Ute!\" Divino cried. \"It\'s us, we\'ve come back.\"

Instead of rushing out to smother them in embraces, Dona Ute called out:
\"It\'s about time. You must have collected enough wood to last a year.
Bring it in right now if you want supper tonight.\"

The door opened and Maricel, one of the smaller children, came out. When
she saw them she exclaimed: \"Divino, you\'ve got a horse!\"

\"A horse?\" Dona Ute echoed. \"Divino, what have you been up to? What
horse?\"

\"Platero is a donkey, not a horse,\" Divino said, and Platero brayed to
prove it.

Sergio walked into the house and stood behind Don Ute, who was cleaning
vegetables in the kitchen sink. Zeca sat at the table peeling potatoes.
The other children were upstairs making their beds and when they heard
Maricel say that Divino had a horse they dropped what they had been
doing and came pouring downstairs to see it, whatever it was.

Platero, accustomed as he was to roam the palace at Maxanatara, followed
Sergio into the house still bearing Divino, who had to duck his head as
they crossed the threshold. Mireya remained outside, too shy to enter
without being asked.

Dona Ute dropped the head of cabbage she was holding and stared
open-mouthed at Divino in his tararobe astride Platero.

\"You waited for us,\" Sergio said.

\"We couldn\'t very well cook without firewood,\" she answered,
understanding something quite different. \"What have you two been up to
and where did you find that donkey and those dresses?\"

The other children, pushing into the kitchen, giggled.

\"We found the Magic Mound,\" Divino began, \"then we fell asleep and
when we woke up Mara was there and she took us to the palace. We didn\'t
want to because we knew you\'d be worried but she made us go and\...\"

As Divino babbled on, still sitting on Platero\'s back, the others not
understanding a word he said, Sergio went outside to fetch Mireya. He
took her hand and led her into the house. They couldn\'t get into the
kitchen, which was so full it looked ready to burst, so they went into
the living-room.

\"I don\'t understand it,\" Sergio said to Mireya. \"We\'ve been gone a
whole month and they act as though it were only a few hours.\"

\"It must be because of the vitaluz,\" Mireya said. \"You and Divino
drank some, didn\'t you?\"

\"Yes, Mamara gave it to us on the first day.\"

\"And that was a full-moon day, like now. Don\'t you see, Sergio? You
and Divino haven\'t gotten any older in a month. Time stood still for
you.\"

\"Then the month in Maxa has only been a few hours here.\"

\"Not even that. The only time that passed here is what it took you to
get to the mound and back.\"

\"And you?\" Sergio asked Mireya. \"How long have you been away?\"

\"When I first arrived in Maxa it was not yet full moon, so I didn\'t
drink any vitaluz until the next full moon, which was about two weeks
later.\"

\"So you\'ve only been away for two weeks.\" Sergio thought a moment.
\"It\'s kind of weird, isn\'t it?\"

At that moment Dona Ute pushed her way out of the kitchen. \"Sergio,\"
she called, \"Where is Sergio?\" She stopped short when she saw Mireya.
Then she smiled. \"Hello, who are you?\"

\"This is Mireya,\" Sergio said. \"We rescued her in Maxalabora. I mean
we found her there, she didn\'t really need to be rescued.\"

\"I\'m beginning to wish that someone would rescue *me*,\" Dona Ute said
under her breath.

\"Mireya would like to go to the little school,\" Sergio went on. \"You
see, she...\"

\"Just a minute now,\" Dona Ute interrupted him. \"First let\'s get some
order in this house. \"Divino,\" she called, \"Get that donkey
outside.\"

Divino slid painfully off Platero\'s back and limped into the
living-room followed by the donkey and the rest of the children. Zeca,
totally bewildered, brought up the rear.

\"But Ute,\" Divino pleaded, \"Platero is used to being in houses.\"

\"Not this one. Out with him!\"

Platero walked calmly up to Don Ute and gently nudged her with his nose.
His meaning was clear: \"Please let me stay.\"

In spite of herself, Dona Ute had to laugh. \"Well, all right, just this
once. Now everybody please sit down. Sergio, I want you to tell us
calmly and clearly exactly what happened from the moment you left this
house with Divino to fetch firewood - which you didn\'t bring, by the
way - until now. Where and how you found this beautiful girl and this
charming indoors donkey\" - they all laughed at that - \"and those
strange robes. Why they are lovely, aren\'t they?\"

They all sat in a circle on the floor - except Platero, or course, who
remained standing - and Sergio told them the whole story of their
adventures in the land of Maxa.

Dona Ute and Zeca were incredulous at first as they listened (the
children believed every word right from the beginning because children
have less trouble believing than grown-ups do), but when he finished and
asked if Mireya could go to the little school, they had to believe him.
They knew that Sergio did not lie and they had Mireya, Platero and the
marvelous tararobes before their eyes as evidence.

After breakfast the next morning a messenger arrived with a letter from
Dona Renata, a teacher at the little school. Dona Ute read the note and
thought for a long time. When she realized that they were all looking at
her, she laughed and said, \"Well, it looks as though one of Tamoshot\'s
friends in Brazil has been at work.

\"What do you mean, Dona Ute?\" Sergio asked excitedly.

\"Renata writes that a man came to the favela yesterday. He told her
that he and some friends had heard about our work and want to help. He
said that money would be made available, and he wants to talk to me as
soon as possible because he will only be in Brazil for a few days.\" She
sighed. \"It looks like the end of my vacation.\"

\"But aren\'t you happy?\" Divino asked.

\"Of course, I was only joking. But it\'s very strange and I\'ll believe
it when I see it.\"

So Dona Ute went back to the favela with the messenger. When the others
returned a few days later they were anxious to know about the mysterious
man who wanted to help.

\"He was tall, with a beard, and walked with a slight stoop,\" Dona Ute
told them. \"He seemed to be sad about something. But he was very
friendly and wanted to know all about our work in the favela.\"

The man gave Dona Ute a large sum of money and when she tried to thank
him he wouldn\'t listen, saying the money wasn\'t his, that he only took
care of it for others. Then he left.

\"That\'s what we wished for,\" Divino said. \"Did you ask if he knew
Tamoshot?\"

\"No,\" Dona Ute replied, \"it didn\'t seem to be the right thing to
ask.\"

### EPILOG

It would be tempting to say, \"And they lived happily ever after.\" But
we do not know if that will be true. All this happened quite recently
and our friends are still living their lives, often happily, but not
always, for life is not always happy, especially if you happen to live
in a favela.

A clinic, a nursery and a new kindergarten were built in the favela and
there was still money left over. Dona Ute and the other teachers decided
to use it to start a little school in another favela. So now there are
little schools in two favelas and more teachers are being trained to
work in them. Dona Ute is happy that they are now able to help more poor
children and their parents. But sometimes she is sad when she thinks of
all those poor people in the world who she cannot help.

Mireya attends the little school, where she is very popular with the
teachers and the other children. She must also attend a regular school
where she is sometimes unhappy because it is so different from her
little school in the favela.

Dona Ute\'s house had no room for another person, let alone a donkey. So
Zeca, with Sergio\'s and Divino\'s help, built a small house with two
rooms in the backyard. One room is for Mireya and the other, a stable
really, is for Platero.

Besides being one of the family, Platero is also the little school\'s
official mascot. He grazes and suns himself in the little school\'s
garden while the children are learning inside. All the children have
ridden him or at least sat on his back. They also draw pictures of him.
Some are very good and others are more funny than good. Platero doesn\'t
mind though, for he doesn\'t think much of pictures anyway.

Of Platero we can truly say: And he lived happily ever after.
