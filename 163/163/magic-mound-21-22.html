Chapter 21

Danger in the Palace

They headed straight for the palace and reached its outskirts after dark on the third day. As they got closer Alaram's mood changed. When Sergio asked him why he suddenly seemed so preoccupied, he said that the animals were trying to warn him that something was wrong.

"I don't know what," he explained. "The animals can't really speak, but they can express things like..."

"Like what?" Mireya asked when he hesitated.

"Like danger."

"Danger?" Sergio repeated. He stopped walking forward and the others stopped with him. "We better be careful. Alaram, let's go on ahead to scout and leave the others here until we find out what the danger may be."

"What about me?" Divino asked.

"Come along. You can be our messenger," Sergio said.

"I will go too," Alto said.

"No, Alto," Alaram told him, "you will be in charge here."

Mireya started to say something but Sergio asked her to stay behind. He explained that if something was wrong at the palace a small group would be less likely to be discovered. His intuition told him that the danger, if it existed, involved Barlach.

Sergio, Alaram and Divino made their way silently through the forest until they saw lights in the distance. A bit farther on they realized that the lights were small fires spread around the palace clearing. They crept up to the edge of the forest and lay still, watching and listening.

The fires turned out to be campfires, at each of which a group of Barlach's soldiers was eating and drinking. The smell of burnt flesh and alcohol wafted towards them.

"They have killed animals to eat them," Alaram whispered sadly. "The animals of Maxanatara are so trusting that it must have been easy to catch them."

"Let's try to hear what they're saying," Sergio said, also whispering.

"They're drunk, there won't be much sense in what they say."

"Sh," Sergio insisted, "listen."

A soldier at the campfire nearest them belched loudly and laughed. "This boar meat beats goat."

"Yeah, but not by much," his companion said. "Too bad that deer was too quick for us."

"The women weren't too quick though."

"We don't have them yet. They're all in the palace."

"Don't worry about that. Barlach and his son Barlachí are in the palace taking care of everything. He promised we could take them back to Urmaxa with us."

Their talk was interspersed with coarse laughter, oaths, drinking and shouting. The soldiers' weapons, heavy maces and long knives, lay strewn on the ground near them. Luckily, firearms were not yet invented in Maxa. Sergio asked Alaram if his men had weapons.

"Every man has a knife, but they are not weapons. We use them for cutting branches, whittling and preparing food."

Sergio sighed. "They wouldn't be much good against those maces. We'll have to think of something else."

"Please think fast, Sergio," Alaram urged. "Mamara, Mara and the rest of the women are in there with Barlach and his son. Something terrible might be happening. We must do something."

"Yes, but what?" Sergio thought and turned to Divino. "Go back and tell the men to come, but carefully, without making any noise. Tell Alto that Barlach and his soldiers are here."

"Right," and Divino was off, slipping silently through the forest as though he had been born in it.

It occured to Sergio that it would be better if Platero and Mireya stayed behind, the donkey because he might make noise and Mireya to keep her out of danger. But it was too late, he dared not call out to Divino.

"What will we do when the men get here?" Alaram asked Sergio. He was restless and impatient.

"I don't know yet, I'm thinking," was the best answer Sergio could muster.

When after about five minutes Sergio added nothing to his last statement, Alaram could restrain himself no longer. "I'm going into the palace," he said. "There's a secret entrance on the west side."

Sergio tried to dissuade him. "They'll see you cross the clearing. You wont stand a chance."

"There's always a chance," Alaram retorted, his jaw set firmly. It was the first time he had gone against Sergio's advice. "I'll try to slip between those two groups." He pointed to where there was a relatively large space between two of the campfires.

"If you do get through and into the palace, remember that Arman and Armaní are in there, surely armed and probably with soldiers. What can you do against them?"

"I'm not doing anything here." Alaram looked at Sergio. They were both stretched out on their stomachs. "I'm not so sure that there are more soldiers inside. Barlach is not interested in the women. Oh, he would take them to Urmaxa to keep his men quiet, but he wants something else."

"What?" Sergio asked, and knew the answer before Alaram could reply: "The vitaluz formula."

Alaram rose to a crouch. "The moon will rise soon, I must go now."

"I'll go with you," Sergio said.

"No, you wait here for the men and tell them what to do."

"If I only knew," Sergio said to himself.

Alaram made his way along the edge of the forest until Sergio could no longer see him. After what seemed much longer than the few minutes that actually passed, he saw him emerge from the forest and run, crouched low, toward the palace. He had to pass between two groups of soldiers whose campfires dimly illuminated the ground over which he had to go. Just as he was midway between the two groups a soldier from one of the groups stood up and turned his way. Alaram saw him from the corner of his eye and dropped to the ground. Sergio held his breath.

The soldier yawned, walked a few paces towards Alaram, picked up a crooked branch from the ground and returned to his campfire. He threw the branch on the fire and sat down again. A moment later Alaram sprinted forwarded until he disappeared into the darkness surrounding the palace.

Sergio let out his breath. Alaram could be right about there not being more soldiers in the palace, Sergio thought, because Barlach would not want them to find out the secret formula. Still, he did not have much hope for gentle Alaram's chances against Arman and Armaní.

Chapter 22

The Death of Barlach

Alaram felt his way along the palace wall until he found a stone that protruded slightly from its surface. Just below it he took hold of an iron ring he knew was there. He twisted it counter-clockwise three times and pulled. A narrow door squeaked open on rusty hinges. Alaram turned to look at the soldiers, but they were making so much noise that they couldn't possibly have heard the door. He bent over and entered through the low threshold, leaving the door ajar so a bit of light from the stars and the campfires could enter the pitch-black, dank tunnel in which he found himself.

Alaram felt along the wall of the tunnel until he came to a wooden panel at its end. A thin line of light flickered at the bottom of the panel, indicating that someone was in the room on the other side. He put his ear against the panel and heard a voice he had never heard before, but knew must belong to Barlach. It was not gruff as he had expected, but soft and oily.

"You are very stubborn, my dear queen. I am offering you everything you could possibly want, even myself. Perhaps you do not understand." There was a long pause during which Alaram tried to imagine what was happening.

Barlach continued. "You would be my queen, nothing less. We would reign over Maxa and all would do out bidding. And if Alaram and his men do not want the Vitaluz, we will let them die, naturally. It would be their choice. Now, be honest, is that not a generous offer?"

"No" Queen Mamara answered firmly. "You are a liar and always have been."

"But my dear lady, what choice do you have? If you don't tell me the secret formula I will kill you."

"Then you will never know the formula."

"Quite right, I am glad you brought that little problem to my attention. First I will kill Mara, and if you still are not convinced I will send my men to Maxalabora to kill your darling Mireya."

"Don't tell him," Alaram heard Mara cry. "I would rather die that have to live with this pig Barlachí."

Alaram heard a loud slap and Barlach saying quickly, "Not yet, Barlaachí, your time will come. Have patience."

"I'll show her who is a pig," Barlachí shouted, obviously in a rage.

Alaram could stand it no longer. He took his knife from an inside pocket of his tarakilt and backed away from the panel in preparation for kicking it open and charging into the room.

Outside, Divino dropped breathlessly at Sergio's side. "I'm back," he said.

"And the men?"

"Yes."

Sergio looked back and saw the men in the forest behind him. He signaled with his arms that they should spread out. "Did Mireya and Platero stay behind?" he asked Divino.

Divino looked around, puzzled. "They started out with us, but I don't see them now."

"They must have decided to stay back there after all," Sergio said, relieved that they would be out of danger.

"Where's Alaram?" Divino asked.

"In the palace."

"What do we do now, Sergio?"

"I'm not sure. They greatly outnumber us and are better armed. But we have the element of surprise on our side and that's very important."

"Yes, it is," Divino agreed without enthusiasm. He was dismayed at the thought of attacking Barlach's soldiers - two boys and a group of gentle Maxanians.

"We could wait until they're asleep, then we'd have a better chance," Sergio said.

"That sounds like a good idea," Divino said, relieved that the attack had been delayed, "and maybe Alaram will be back by then."

"Yes, Maybe." So they settled down to wait.

Alaram was about to kick the panel open when he heard Mamara say: "If you are the God's son, why do you have to ask me for the vitaluz formula? Your sister, Light, gave it to us."

Barlach laughed his cruel laugh. "The god? Who has seen him?"

"What do you mean?"

"The god has disappeared. He is as good as dead, so let us not bring him into the picture," Barlach said angrily. "I have had enough of this. Tell me the formula or I will let Barlachí tear your daughter apart limb from limb."

His voice was still calm but it was now hard and cruel. He obviously meant what he said.

Alaram kicked open the wooden panel and sprang into the room with his knife glistening in his right hand. He took in the scene at a glance. Mamara sat rigidly on the huge bed. Mara was kneeling on the floor with her head on her mother's lap. Barlach, a short, aging, ugly man with bloodshot eyes and dark, drooping jowls, lounged in Alaram's armchair across the room. Barlachí stood next to the bed menacingly. He was dark and ugly like his father but nothing about him drooped. He looked strong as a bull. They both wore black jumpsuit uniforms and bore heavy maces. Barlachí held his tightly in both hands. Barlach's was resting in his lap. When Alaram burst into the room he stood up and his mace fell to the floor. That was his fatal mistake. As he bent to pick it up Alaram rushed him. He retrieved the mace and started to lift it over his head but Alaram was like lightning. He dived with his arm outstretched and buried his knife in Barlach's throat. They both fell to the floor from the momentum of Alaram's charge.

Barlachí, recovered from the surprise, rushed at Alaram with a wild swing of his mace. Alaram was still on his knees. He slipped under Barlachí's arm as the mace passed over his head and Barlach's own momentum threw him onto Alaram's knife. But his mace struck the side of Alaram's head, glanced off his shoulder and finally buried three of its spikes in the inside of his thigh. Alaram fell to the floor. His opponent, with a cry of pain and rage, fell dying on top of his father's body.

The moon, almost full, rose abruptly over the eastern horizon. Then it continued its journey more slowly over the tops of the trees, changing from pink to white and spreading its dim gray light over Maxa. The campfires had burned down and the soldiers were sleeping or dozing.

Sergio sent Divino to tell the men to gather up the soldiers' weapons while they slept. They should have their knives ready in case they were discovered. He hoped it was the right thing to do. He could see no alternative. If they wanted to defeat Barlach and rescue the women, they had to do it now while the soldiers were groggy from drinking. In the morning it would be too late. But what if the soldiers woke up and discovered them? They might all be killed because of his mistake.

"If only Tamoshot were here," he said aloud to himself.

"He is here, Sergio."

Recognizing Mireya's voice, he twisted over onto his back and beheld a sight he would never forget.

Mireya stood alongside Platero holding his silver collar. Astride the white donkey sat a transfigured Tamoshot. He wore a white tararobe and white taraslippers. His hair, instead of looking like a scraggly mop, fell over his neck in silvery waves. His beard seemed luminous and his eyes sparkled like blue diamonds.

"The men should enter the clearing with their knives held up, but they should not attack," he said to Sergio in a voice full of authority. Sergio stood up and entered the clearing. He stretched his arms out and motioned the men to advance. From the forest the twenty-three men stepped out and strode a few paces into the clearing. Sergio turned his palms inward, holding the men back. He had no idea what would happen next.

From the corner of his eye he saw Platero emerge from the forest bearing Tamoshot. They passed him and the arc of Maxanians and advanced beyond the smoldering campfires. At a point midway between the palace and the campfires Platero turned to face the soldiers. The moonlight streaming over the white-clad man and the white donkey gave them a powerful, unearthly aspect. The few soldiers who had not fallen asleep saw them and cried out like wounded animals. Suddenly they were all awake and on their feet in a state of fear and confusion.

"What is it?" cried one.

"He rides the monster?" said another.

"It must be the god."

"Barlach says the god is dead."

Tamoshot raised his right hand and they were silent.

He spoke: "Barlach is dead."

This was received in shocked silence.

"Go back to Urmaxa!" Tamoshot's voice was like thunder.

The soldiers turned towards their weapons and saw Sergio with his arms still outstretched flanked by Divino and Mireya and the men with their knives upraised like the pearls of a necklace around the edge of the forest.

"Leave your weapons!" Tamoshot commanded.

One of the soldiers ran to the palace, around it and into the forest behind it. The rest followed, fleeing in panic in the direction of Urmaxa. The clearing in front of the palace was soon free of soldiers, their dying campfires smoldering in the moonlight and their weapons left strewn on the ground like useless junk.

Sergio ran to Tamoshot and said, "Alaram went into the palace to save Queen Mamara and Mara. Barlach and Barlachí are in there."

Tamoshot slowly dismounted.

"Is Barlach really dead?" Divino asked him.

"We shall soon see." Tamoshsot headed for the palace gate and the others followed him. When they reached it he called out:

"Open the gate." A few moments passed before the huge gate began to swing open. Inside the women gave startled cries of joy and rushed out to greet their men. Tamoshot, followed by Sergio, Mireya and Divino, walked across the courtyard and into the palace.

The scene in the royal bedroom was one of turmoil and grief. Barlach and Barlachí lay sprawled on the floor. The two women had managed to lift King Alaram's body onto the bed and were crying disconsolately over him. They barely looked up when the group entered the room.

Tamoshot stood at the foot of the bed. "Why do you weep?" he asked the women.

Mara lifted her tear-stained face and said, "Can't you see? My father is dead."

"He is not dead," Tamoshot said softly.

"He is, he has no heartbeat."

"Listen again, my child."

Mara lowered her head to Alaram's chest and listened.

"Mother," she cried, "his heart is beating now, he is alive."

"His leg is badly wounded," Tamoshot said. "It will be a long time before he can walk again and he will always limp. Barlach's mace was poisoned, so you must clean the wound carefully and regularly until it heals. And give him this potion, Mamara, a drop a day." He took a vial from beneath his tararobe and handed it to her. "You should also drink a drop a day if the cure is to be effected."

Mamara looked at the vial and then at Alaram, who was still very pale but whose breast had begun to slowly rise and fall. She bent over him and placed her hand on his warm forehead.

"Yes," she whispered, "yes, I will, and we will both be cured."

Tamoshot walked out of the room and motioned for Sergio and the others to follow him. Outside the palace gate the women and men were so intent on hugging each other and talking all at once that they did not notice the little man in white, the three children and the white donkey pass among them and disappear into the forest.
