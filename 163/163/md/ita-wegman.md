# The Artificial Anthroposophical Society -- a continuation

## by Frank Thomas Smith

In <https://southerncrossreview.org/161/artificial-society.html> *The
Artificial Anthroposophical Society* I describe the current situation of
the problematic General Anthropsophical Society, without recommending a
possible solution. Why not? Because I knew of none. Now, however, after
some further study, certain concepts which I would like to share with
you have fallen into my lap, so to speak.

In February 1935 Ita Wegman wrote to Maria Röschl:

"All the old forms, also the very last one for Anthroposophy (the
Christmas Meeting Society 1923) have been basically broken, and it seems
to me now that one should no longer seek a form for the life of
Anthroposophy, but that every individual is the form with whom
Anthroposophy wants unite. Where this is the case, others will find it
and join to become members of the true spiritual association. The
Anthroposophical Society is no longer necessary because Anthroposophy is
already on the earth. It depends now on the individuals and they must
together form a higher association, by means of their own development,
that has its roots in the spiritual world..."

In March 1935 she wrote in a letter to Herbert Hahn:

"With the Christmas Meeting Dr. Steiner anticipated an organization that
was not possible to implement because the old forces did not yet permit
it ... Perhaps a future form can be possible once some people free
themselves from the Society."\
<sub>From Peter Selg "Geistiger Widerstand und Überwindung" Ita Wegman 1925-1933, Dornach 2005</sub>

From Gerhard von Beckerath in his book "*Rudolf Steiners Leidensweg"
2011*:

"Today as Rudolf Steiner's student I must call to *all,* not just a few
of Rudolf Steiner's students: Yes, it has now become certain that in
1935 Ita Wegman correctly assessed what the possible form the
anthroposophical movement on earth should take today. In my opinion, we
must have the courage to truly perceive the realities that are becoming
ever more apparent and to act accordingly, so that not only a few but
all of Rudolf Steiner's students sooner or later become free of the
Society -- instead of continuing being driven!

Or do we, by the persistence of outdated but ingrained habits of
thought, continue to believe that Rudolf Steiner's "shattered" social
experiment of 1923 with the Christmas Conference can still be practiced
by us today -- even without Rudolf Steiner? ....... Have we not learned
from painful experience to recognize the best possible form for the
anthroposophical movement on earth to take today without Rudolf Steiner?
With truthfulness, honesty, modesty and patience individually achieved.
Instead of simply maintaining the current form of the Society after
Rudolf Steiner's death, with its many demands and *centrally* oriented
structure, a social confederative organism [^1] could be formed on the
basis of autonomous local associations of people striving for
anthroposophy. This would no longer demand more than what we can achieve
in the foreseeable future....The local associations could then join
together into Country-Associations. (Country Societies already exist.)
Together these would form a World-Association that has its active center
in the Goetheanum in Dornach, without attempting to implement the
Christmas Meeting structure. The various Country-Associations would have
the duty to maintain the Goetheanum, which would continue to house the
Free University for Spiritual Science..."

I will now speak for myself: In English and in most other languages
beyond the Germanic ones, the name "Free School for Spiritual Science"
is used, although the original German reads Freie *Hochschule* für
Gesteswissenschaft -- "Hochschule" means "University". Perhaps we say
"School" because it would be embarrassing to call something a university
which is not one at all. A solution to this mis-translation would be to
make it correct: turn the *Hochschule* into a *real* university, with a
curriculum of subjects of study resulting in various degrees: literary,
scientific, professional, etc. and, of course, spiritual
science/Anthroposophy. Courses could be in various languages, depending
on the Professors' ability and wishes, with emphasis on English to make
the university accessible to students of many nationalities.

[^1]: A confederation is a union of independent associations that work
 together for a common purpose.
