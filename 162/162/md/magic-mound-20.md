### Chapter 20

### Leave not your Task Undone

The group of twenty-four men, two boys, one girl and one white donkey made its
way single file through the forest, more like a jungle in this part of Maxa,
towards the Riovita. Alaram led the way followed by Sergio, then Mireya, Divino,
Platero and the rest of the men.

There was no trail, so the long file twisted and turned like a dragon with a
long tail. At times they were blocked on all sides by vegetation, so Alto
stepped forward and cut through with powerful swipes of his machete-like knife.
It was difficult to converse, strung out as they were, but not impossible if
they spoke loudly enough. This seemed to be a general desire and the result was
a din which rivaled that of the monkeys at twilight.

They moved along quickly and happily, relying on Alaram\'s unerring sense of
direction, and reached the river at sundown of the second day. The next morning
they found the raft tied to a tree on the river-bank, moss-covered but intact
and seaworthy. Sergio noted that it was sturdily built and even handsome. It was
obvious to him that Alaram was an excellent craftsman. The river was wider and
swifter here than in the north where they had crossed it running.

\"Why don\'t we just run across?\" Divino suggested.

Sergio looked at the river rushing before them and at the other bank far away.
\"No, that can only be done at that one place, probably. In any case, I
wouldn\'t want to try it here.\" He smiled. \"And why should we when we have
this beautiful raft?\"

The raft was large, but now with Sergio, Divino and Platero as additional
passengers, it was clearly impossible for all of them to cross the river
together. Sergio suggested to Alaram that he assign the four strongest men as
oarsmen, and divide the rest into two groups and go across in two trips.

Sergio, Divino, Mireya and Platero went across on the second trip together with
a small group of Maxanians. They held the raft against the river bank for
Platero to board. The donkey stood on unsteady legs in the middle of the raft
and felt that it had been easier to fly across the chasm on air than cross the
river on a raft.

They landed in Maxanatara downstream from the first group because of the
river\'s current. They tied the raft to a tree and made their way to where
Alaram and his men sat talking excitedly about their return to the palace and
their women.

Sergio asked Alaram where they were and how long a journey lay before them.
Alaram explained that they were about three days journey southwest of the
palace.

\"And the mound?\" Sergio asked.

Alaram looked at him searchingly. \"Yes, what about it?\"

\"Where is it from here?\"

Alaram picked up a twig and scratched and X in the ground. \"We are here,\" he
said. Then he scratched another X above and to the right of the first one.
\"This is the palace.\" Finally he drew and O midway between the two Xs and to
the right of them.

\"And this is the mound.\"

Sergio took another twig and drew a line from the X which represented their
present position to the O and another line from the O to the second X.

\"If we went this way,\" he said, indicating the detour, \"It wouldn\'t take you
very far out of your way, would it?\"

\"Sergio,\" Alaram said, \"Won\'t you come with us to the palace and explain
everything to Mamara and the rest of the women?\"

\"But Alaram, you have already shown that you can explain much better than I
could. Divino and I must go home.\"

\"Yes, Dona Ute will be worried,\" Divino said, not for the first time during
the adventure.

\"I will go to Brazil, too,\" Mireya said, \"with Platero.\"

Sergio had been hoping that Mireya would go with them and now he smiled at her.
\"You never told us where you\'re from in Brazil, Mireya, or how you found the
mound.\"

The girl looked at her hands folded in her lap and didn\'t answer, so Sergio
added, \"Where will you go in Brazil?\"

Mireya decided that she must tell them her story, and she began: \"My family and
I lived in the north where we were very poor\-\--\"

Divino interrupted: \"We\'re from the north, too, at least Sergio is, I was born
in Sao Paulo. And we\'re poor too, right Sergio?\"

\"Then you know how it is there -- hunger, sickness, poverty, except for the
rich of course, but they are very few.\"

\"I don\'t really remember,\" Sergio said. \"I was too small when we left, but
most of the people in the favela come from the north and they told us about
it.\"

\"My parents sold our rancho and everything else we owned, except for the goat,
who was old and skinny and gave no milk, so my mother gave her to an old lady
who was even poorer than we.\" Mireya paused, remembering. \"We took a bus, I
don\'t know how many days it took us to reach Sao Paulo. When we arrived we went
to the favela, where my father had relatives who let us sleep on the floor of
their shack -- there were four children, my three older brothers and me -- until
we could find a place of our own. Soon my father bought a shack in the same
favela. He paid almost all the money he had received for the rancho. It was a
miserable shack, nothing like our rancho, but at least we didn\'t have to sleep
on the floor.\"

\"Not long afterward my mother became ill and died. I don\'t know what illness
she had or how long she had it. One day she took to her bed and died without
even having seen a doctor.\"

Mireya\'s voice broke, but she did not cry, not outwardly at least.

\"My father had always been a drinker, but after Mamá died he was drunk most of
the time and didn\'t work. I can\'t really blame him for that because there was
very little work to be had. Then he sent my brothers out to beg on the streets,
only there were so many beggars they didn\'t bring much home and what they did
bring my father used to buy wine. Then my father\'s friend said they should
steal instead of beg because they would make more money that way. He offered to
show them how and my father agreed. My mother would never have allowed them to
steal, but she was no longer there to prevent it. The friend said I should steal
too, because people did not expect girls to steal, so we could get away with
more than boys could. I didn\'t want to, but my father said I must.\"

Alaram brushed the tears from his eyes. He had never heard anything like this
before and it made him very sad. Sergio and Divino had heard many similar
stories, including their own, which was not much different from Mireya\'s, so
they only nodded.

\"I ran away,\" Mireya went on. \"I didn\'t know where to go so I wandered the
streets hunting for bit of food in the garbage. One day a man spoke to me. He
said I could go with him to Paraty. I didn\'t know where Paraty was and I
didn\'t like the man, who was dirty and looked cruel, but I knew I would die if
I stayed on the streets. So I went with him to his shack in Paraty, where I
found that he didn\'t want to help me, only to have me beg and steal for _him_.

She looked over her shoulder where Platero stood sleeping. \"He had a donkey,
Platero. Probably he stole him, for he is a very good donkey and nothing else
about that man was good. He had no name really, I gave him his name.\"

\"Dona Ute told us a story about Platero,\" Divino broke in.

\"I\'d like to hear it sometime,\" Mireya said, smiling at Divino.

\"Go on, Mireya,\" Sergio said.

\"He used Platero to pull a heavy trash cart and beat him when he couldn\'t pull
huge piles of junk he found or stole.\"

\"How awful,\" Alaram said.

\"Yes, so I ran away from that man too, and took Platero with me. One morning we
left before dawn while the man was still sleeping. We walked out of Paraty into
the country and up a mountain, then into a forest. I was very tired and hungry
when we found the mound...\"

\"The magic mound,\" Divino interrupted.

\"Yes, but I didn\'t know that it was magic then. For me it was only a clean
cool place to rest. So Platero and I climbed up the mound and fell asleep.\"
Mireya paused and smiled. She was beautiful when she smiled.

\"When I awoke the next morning the sun was streaming through the tree branches
onto my face. Mara called to me from the bottom of the mound and we went down to
her and she led us to the palace.\" She looked at them even more brightly. \"And
here I am.\"

\"But Mireya,\" Alaram said, \"why do you want to go back to such a life?\"

The lovely girl blushed and started to answer twice before she found the right
words the third time. \"I thought it might be possible for me to\...for me to go
with Sergio and Divino to their school.\"

\"Oh Mireya,\" Dvino cried, \"that\'s a great idea!\"

\"From what you\'ve told me the school is something very special, not like the
schools I went to. And your Dona Ute, I feel like I know her already.\"

Until then Sergio had had the impression that Mireya was, well, not a princess
exactly because there are no princesses in Brazil, but at the very least a fine
girl from a rich family. That she was poor like him had never entered his mind.
He was excited and happy at the prospect of her going to their school. He
noticed that Mireya was looking at him expectantly.

\"I think it\'s a great idea, too,\" he said. \"We must ask Dona Ute first and
find a place for you to live, but I\'m sure she will agree, that she\'ll want
you in the school, Mireya.\"

Alaram tried to convince Mireya to stay in Maxanatara, but halfheartedly, for he
saw in her radiant eyes that there was no chance of her changing her mind. Then
he turned to Sergio:

\"They won\'t believe me. You have no idea of the bitterness which developed
between Mamara and me. But if you go with us and explain everything and Mireya
confirms what you say, then Mamara will believe. Otherwise\...\" Alaram\'s eyes
pleaded more eloquently than his words.

\"It would only be a few more days,\" Mireya said to Sergio.

\"Dona Ute will be worried about us,\" Divino said.

\"Yes, Divino,\" Sergio said, \"but what would she think of us if we left the
job unfinished?\"

\"We rescued\...I mean we found Mireya.\"

\"Yes, but there is more to the task than that. Do you remember the last line of
the Raven\'s verse?\"

Divino thought a while, then said:

_Leave not your task undone._

He was afraid that Queen Mamara would try to keep them in Maxanatara, but his
brother and Mireya seemed to feel that to go back to the palace and convince the
women that they would live again after they died was the right thing to do. And
the last line of the raven\'s verse, which he had just recited, left little room
for argument, so he agreed. Alaram was overjoyed and his happiness was so
contagious that even Divino was glad of their decision.
