# My Incarnation That Was\...or not

## by Frank Thomas Smith

It was fifty-eight years ago that I wrote a children's poem, *The Little
Plant*:

> *In the heart of a seed, buried deep, so deep,*\
> *A dear little plant lay fast asleep.*\
> *Wake! said the sunshine, and creep to the light*\
> *Wake! said the voice of the raindrops bright.*\
> *The little plant heard and rose up to see*\
> *What the wonderful outside world* might be.

At that time, 1968, I had co-founded a Waldorf School in a suburb of
Buenos Aires, Argentina. It started with 3 grades: 1^st^, 2^nd^ and
3^rd^. The School was named San Miguel Arcángel.

During the time between 1968 and 1974, I always gave this poem to new
1^st^ grades. The children seemed all very happy with the poem and so
was I.

Once I received a Waldorf School magazine from the US. In it was this
poem and it said "Author unknown". I thought someone had picked up my
poem somewhere and the magazine published it. So I sent the magazine a
note saying I was the author. They never answered.

In 1974 I was transferred from Argentina to zurich, Switzerland by my
international company and after four years I was transferred again, this
time to Frankfurt, Germany. Six years later I was once more transferred
this time to Geneva, Switzerland. And after two years in Geneva, I was
transferred back to Argentina.

Once there, I was disappointed to learn that there were still only two
Waldorf Schools in Argentina. This was because there were hardly any
Waldorf teachers. There is no point in founding a school if there are no
teachers. So I organized and co-founded a Waldorf Teacher's Seminar in
Buenos Aires. It became successful very quickly because there was much
interest in Waldorf education in general and there were many people
interested in becoming Waldorf teachers. The Waldorf School movement
began to grow quickly after that moment.

In 1999 I started publishing an online magazine,
*SouthernCrossReview.org, a Review of Anthroposophy, fiction,
education, science, current events, essays, book reviews, poetry.*

At one point, I published *The Little Plant* in the Poetry Section of
the Review. Someone contacted me saying that I was not the author. I
wrote back to him saying that he was mistaken, that I had written the
poem in Buenos Aires, Argentina, sometime between 1968 and 1974. He was
not giving up. He wrote back again with an attachment with the photo of
the poem, the exact same poem I had written. The author was listed as
Kate L. Brown. I was astonished when I checked the internet and found
that, just by writing the name of the poem, it appeared as having been
written by Kate L. Brown.

Kate Brown had lived in Massachusetts near Boston. She died in 1921,
eleven years before I was born. I didn't think then of how she and I
could have written the same identical poem. All I know is that she was a
school teacher and she wrote many books for and about how to educate
children. It was only afterwards that I thought well, why not, but only
if I was her reincarnation.

Well, you see, that sounds crazy, but when you take into account that I
have been an anthroposophist for many years and for anthroposophists
reincarnation is a fact of life and death, it may not sound quite so crazy.

Rudolf Steiner said that in our times more and more people will be
experiencing their previous incarnation. Ay, there's the rub! I have not
experienced ever being Kate L, Brown. Our only link is The Little Plant.
So perhaps there's another explanation. If you can think of something
please let me know.
