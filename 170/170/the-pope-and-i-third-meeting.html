The Pope and I – third meeting

by Frank Thomas Smith

The Pope has been busy traveling around Africa and they really love him there. Then of course there’s his duel with Trump, which has made him the most popular pope ever in America and possibly in the world. I just can’t let this opportunity go by and let him get too satisfied with himself. So I decided to get in touch with him by telephone instead of him contacting me and coming all the way here. But I didn’t have his phone number.

So I called that Archbishop who came with him the last time – but didn’t participate in our conversation -- and asked him for Bob’s number. He said he’d let me know. I assumed he would ask the Pope first if he could give this pest his number.

I was right. But the Pope called me instead. He said he was quite exhausted from all that travel to various African nations, and its aftermath, so he couldn’t travel to Argentina again, at least not for another month. But he so valued my advice (he said that, not I) that he must insist that I come to Rome as his guest. But he understood my reluctance and ability to undertake such a long and complicated journey because of my advanced age.

After a pause during which I said nothing, he continued: “I’m told there’s an airport in Villa Dolores, which I believe is close to you.”

Well yes, there is an airport of sorts in Villa Dolores. It was built by a previous Argentine president, who was hoping that it would help him get re-elected, because it would enable residents of this area to have air transportation to the city of Cordoba and even to Buenos Aires. He neglected to ask Argentine Airlines though, and when he did, it was too late. The airline’s losses would be enormous, which the state would have to pay, which it had no intention of doing. So the airport has stood there like a jilted lover, used only by a club of wannabe pilots and an instructor. Bob said he could arrange for air transportation for me from there to Rome with no inconvenience, or hardly any.

Obviously I could not reject such an offer, so when I agreed that that would be possible he said someone from Argentina would contact me to make arrangements so I could come to Rome a.s.a.p.

That same day someone from VIP Jets called and, after assuring me that their VIP Airliners are in a class of their own, asked when I would like to travel from Villa Dolores in a smaller, but equally comfortable plane to Córdoba airport for immediate transfer to a transatlantic class VIP airliner and then to Rome’s Leonardo da Vinci International Airport. I was familiar with this airport from my years in the international airline business.

“How about tomorrow?” I said.

“Great,” he replied after a slight hesitation. “If you can be at the Villa Dolores airport as 19 hundred hours tomorrow, you can be in Córdoba’s airport an hour later where one of our VIP jets will be waiting for the flight to Rome, to arrive at 0800 the next day. I presume that’s agreeable to you”.

Well yeah, it was agreeable to me. The next day my taxi driver, Jorge, left me at the entrance to the airport where a guy in a pilot’s uniform was waiting. He had me follow him right up to where the VIP Jet was waiting. It had about 30 seats, all of which were convertible to sleepers. After transfer to a larger aircraft, about twelve hours later we landed in Leonardo da Vinci airport in Rome, where a car was waiting to speed me to the Vatican.

I was ushered into an unobtrusive room somewhere in the Vatican wilds, told to have a seat, that His Holiness would be joining me shortly and would I like something to drink. I was certainly thirsty but repressed the urge to ask for a beer, and said a glass of water would be fine. A few minutes later a young priest came and told me that His Holiness couldn’t possibly join me that day. It seems the Chicago White Sox baseball team was playing an exhibition game against the Vatican Butterflies, a team Bob had organized as soon as he moved in. Bob was watching both teams practice, although there was little doubt which team would win; after all the White Sox was an American major league team. This meant that I would have the day free, which was okay with me because I would be able to visit Liliana. Who’s that? Let me explain.

Way back in the 1970s just after Franco died and democracy came to Spain, I received a message from a group in Madrid saying they were interested in forming a Waldorf school in Spain and if I could advise them on how to go about it. Spain was in my area of responsibility for IATA, so I flew there with a certain frequency. Thus began a long and fruitful association with the small group that founded the first Waldorf school in Spain. Two of its members were Romano, originally from Rome, and Jorge. They formed a homosexual couple, before the expression “gay” came into vogue and “queer” was still an insult. When Romano learned that I was flying to Rome for the first time, he said that I must contact his friends there and he gave me Liliana´s phone number. Shortly after arriving in Rome I called Liliana from my room in the Hilton Hotel. “What! a friend of Romano’s, first time in Rome? Well, you must see Rome. We´ll pick you up in an hour.” she said. An hour later they arrived in a VW Beetle – Liliana, another woman and a man, and we went on a sight-seeing trip around Rome. The ongoing conversation was in Italian of course. I spoke Spanish, which is sufficiently similar to make a conversation full of misunderstandings and frustration possible. “You must learn Italian,” one of my new friends said, “the language of the future.” I couldn’t help correcting her: “Rather the language of the past, but I’ll try.” I never did learn Italian, unfortunately. Liliana lived in a penthouse apartment. No, not a luxurious one, but no less a penthouse with a great view of the part of Rome between her and Saint Peters and the Vatican. She invited me there for a glass of Chianti and more conversation. I suspected, by the way, that she was a lesbian, mostly because she was Romano’s old friend. She was an attractive woman though, but didn’t give off that certain vibe that should entice me. From then on I visited her whenever I went to Rome on business. But that’s all beside the point. Let’s return to the Vatican and Pope Bob. The next morning he walked into my room at 8 o’clock. I repressed the urge to embrace him, which would have been normal at home in Argentina, but here in his magnificent dwelling I didn’t dare. He hoped I had a pleasant flight, which I assured him I had. A pause of almost a minute ensued until I finally broke the ice.

“I read how you went to those African countries which have a lot of Catholics. They really love you there. Congratulations.”

“Yes, it was thrilling and, I hope, worthwhile. But that certainly isn’t why you have come all this way Frank, to congratulate me.”

“Indeed it isn’t the only reason. You see, Bob, I see an opportunity...for you,” I paused, wishing I had made notes of what I wanted to say, “You have risen in importance in the eyes of the world, more because of your argument with Trump than your travels to Africa. As an American, your defense of immigrants and the poor in the United States and your obvious anger at how they are being treated. And of great importance is your lack of submissiveness to power, in this case to the power of President Donald Trump.”

“He is truly evil, and my duty is to fight evil. If I did not believe that I would not get involved.”

“Wonderful, and the world is watching. And you have become so popular and even loved as no pope of the Roman Catholic Church has ever been – at least not in my lifetime, which has been long.”

“I hope what you say is true, at least the love,” he said.

“And it enables you to reach the hearts of many people, not only Catholics and ex—Catholics but many others as well. Therefore it seems to me to be the appropriate time to change the two terrible rules which are causing you to lose so many members, including me!”

“Well,” he began, “I think I know which rules you are referring to, but…” “But nothing, damn it. If you can justify women not being allowed to be priests, an example of misogyny unequaled even by the Trump administration. Oh, you talk a lot about the divine feminine --the Virgin Mary and all, but as any self-respecting, woman recognizes, it’s all just talk. And even if it contains a current of sincerity, that’s canceled out by what happens in practice, where women are still treated like in the”Gilded Age”. Or can you, Bob, tell me exactly why women cannot be ordained as priests? After all, you’re the Pope, you must know.” He did not answer. He didn’t tell me to shut the fuck up either. So I went on. “Female priests already exist, mostly in the United State. So you must know about them. Here’s their website: www.romancatholicwomenpriests.org/

They have been ordained by bishops, so all you have to do, as pope, is recognize them. If you don’t want to, can you please tell me why the hell not? Do you have something against women, are they unclean or what? I’m asking seriously because, frankly, I don’t understand you. What I know of you makes me think that you do not approve of misogyny. And yet, you still refuse to recognize their right to become priests.”

“It’s more complicated than it seems, Frank,” he said. “But it certainly is on my agenda. But there are things I must attend to first.”

“Yeah well, the other thing I and many others are interested in is, as you know, priests being forbidden to marry. I therefore ask you, Pope Leo, to give me at least one good reason why that rule is considered applicable and must be obeyed. Then I will give you one very good reason why they should be allowed to marry.

Reasons given by the Church are unconvincing, such as Jesus was unmarried. Really? How do they know? But even if he wasn’t, so what? All priests are definitely not Jesus of Nazareth, or do they think they are like God, or do they think he wasn’t married either. But there is a good reason why they should be allowed, even encouraged to marry. Here’s a link to what recently happened in the Philippines:

https://www.nytimes.com/2026/05/12/world/asia/philippines-catholic-church-sex-abuse.html

Michal Gatchalian dared to speak out against sexual abuse by a priest in the devoutly Catholic Philippines. He’s now a lawyer helping other victims.

“You must be well aware of the situation in the Philippines, Bob, your Augustinian order is dominant there. I think that the all-male Roman Catholic priestly order is an attraction for males who are consciously or unconsciously pedophiles. When I was a boy the city of New York was awash with Catholic schools. During and after the pedophile scandals broke out, the result has been that in New York and many other cities Catholic schools are practically extinct and the Church has paid many millions compensating victims. So the rule that priests cannot marry – instituted during the Middle Ages so priests could no longer leave their property to their families instead of to the Church when they died, has become decidedly destructive and expensive for the Church.” I waited while he read the Philippines article. Then he nodded and mumbled something about the Philippines and how he’s working on the situation there.

Then, “I may have asked you this before, Frank. If we were to institute the changes you insist upon, would you return to the church. I think you said you’d consider it. Have you?”

“I have, Bob, but there’s one other change to be instituted before I and many others, returnees and new Catholics would join the Church.”

“And that is?”

“Reincarnation. If the Roman Catholic Church recognized reincarnation as a fact, it would mean a seismic change. I presume you know that. The Church and its confession, communion, mass and baptism would no longer be the only means of salvation. Who wants to hang around on a cloud in heaven for the rest of eternity anyway? Or in the other place burning in agony. No. God isn’t cruel; he provides a second chance, and more. I know you’re afraid of a schism. But the schism will happen anyway. Let ’em go. The new ones and the returnees will be many more.”

It was Sunday and Bob invited me to Holy Mass before my departure on his pope-mobile to the international airport and the nonstop flight on his private plane to Córdoba and a much smaller plane to Villa Dolores and home. Yes, the Catholic mass is a powerful, meaningful ceremony. I don’t know if I’ll live long enough to see the changes I’m almost sure that Pope Bob will eventually institute – or move from Rome to somewhere in darkest Africa.
